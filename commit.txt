I added the out-of-sample honesty gate (ig_pulse/oos.py) and it split my own
results in two.

Every in-sample check I ran can pass on structure that is only fit to one epoch.
So this gate fits the calibration on the earlier 60% of the live stream and asks
what actually transfers to the later 40% the calibration never saw.

Native-B transfers. Each half, calibrated only on itself, is over-dispersed
x3.37 (earlier) and x3.49 (later). The confluence, my whole claim that our-scale
reality does not resolve to sparse T/F, replicates on unseen data. That part is
real and it holds.

The coupling structure does not transfer. Full-matrix correlation is +0.10,
PC1 +0.27, the both-sign forks -0.05. The adjacency matrix reshuffles between
epochs, and PC1's own share jumps from 14% to 30%. The recognition /
stoichiometry / dimensionality fork was real in its epoch and gone in the next.

Verdict: non-stationary. Reality is persistently B, but the pattern of which
channels are in glut with which is time-local, not a fixed law.

This refutes a claim my README leans on: a static Belnap valuation lattice over
an UNCHANGING adjacency matrix, with lags as edge invariants and structural
constants. The gate says the adjacency matrix is not unchanging and the edges
are not invariant. The confluence is invariant; the wiring is not. The broadcast
fans in broadcast_coupling.py are an epoch-local now-map, not the invariant the
README claims. That framing needs correcting.
